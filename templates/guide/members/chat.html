Chat Channels

Communicate with members, committees, and groups in real time

Quick Actions

Open Chat

1 What are Chat Channels?

Chat channels are persistent, group messaging spaces inside Parliament. They're organized by topic, committee, or group — think of them like group texts that everyone in the relevant group can scroll back through.

🌐 Open

Visible and joinable by any logged-in member. General chapter-wide discussions.

👥 Committee

Automatically accessible to members, chairs, and advisors of a specific committee.

🔒 Restricted

Access granted by officers — by specific user, member type, or officer status.

2 Finding Your Channels

Go to Chat in the navigation. The index shows every channel you have access to — open channels you haven't joined yet appear in a separate "browse" section.

1

Your channels

Channels you're already part of appear at the top, sorted by most recent activity. Unread messages show a badge count.

2

Committee channels

If you're on a committee, that committee's channel is automatically available to you — no joining required.

3

Restricted channels

These won't appear at all unless an officer has granted you access. Contact an officer if you think you should have access to a channel.

3 Sending Messages

Type in the message box at the bottom of any channel and press Enter or click Send. New messages appear in real time for everyone in the channel.

Message actions

  • ✏️ Edit — hover a message you sent to see the edit option. Edited messages are marked.
  • 🗑️ Delete — hover your own message and click delete. Officers can delete any message.

Heads up: Chat messages are visible to everyone with channel access, including officers. Keep conversations appropriate.

4 Read vs. Write Access

Some restricted channels grant read-only access — you can see the messages but can't reply. This is common for announcement-style channels. If your message box is grayed out, you have read-only access.

Read access

You can view all messages and scroll history, but the message box is disabled.

Write access

You can send, edit, and delete your own messages. Officers may also be able to delete others' messages.
