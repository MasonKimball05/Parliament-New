Kai Reports

Understanding the Kai system for conduct reports

Quick Actions

My Kai Dashboard Submit Report

What is Kai?

The Kai system is the chapter's confidential conduct reporting system. It allows members to report concerns about behavior, violations of chapter policies, or other issues that need to be addressed by chapter leadership.

Confidentiality: All Kai reports are handled confidentially by the Kai Committee. The details of reports are not shared publicly.

1 Submitting a Report

Any member can submit a Kai report. Reports can be directed at a specific member or submitted as a general concern.

Report Information

  • Title: Brief description of the concern
  • Category: Type of concern (conduct, policy violation, etc.)
  • Description: Detailed explanation of the incident or concern
  • Targeted Member: Optional - the member the report concerns
  • Supporting Documents: Optional - upload evidence if applicable

What happens next: After you submit a report, the Kai Committee chair(s) are automatically notified by email. They will review the report and begin the deliberation process.

2 Understanding Report Status

You can track the progress of your submitted reports through the Kai Dashboard.

Report Statuses

Archived

Deliberation Outcomes

  • Pending: Still under review
  • Heard: Matter was discussed, no further action
  • Warning Issued: Formal warning given
  • Mediation: Parties worked through resolution
  • Sanctions Applied: Formal consequences imposed
  • Dismissed: Report not substantiated

3 Viewing Your Reports

Your Kai Dashboard shows two types of reports:

Reports You Submitted

Track the status of reports you filed against others or general concerns.

Reports About You

View reports where you are the subject, visible only after deliberation has begun.

Privacy Note: If someone files a report about you, you will only see it after the committee has reviewed it and made an initial determination. This protects the integrity of the review process.

4 Requesting Closure

After a report has been addressed by the committee, parties may request to close the case.

Request Types

  • Request Closure: Ask the committee to close the case after it has been addressed. Available to both the reporter and the accused member.
  • Drop Case (Submitter Only): Request to withdraw your complaint entirely. Only available if sanctions haven't been applied.

Committee Approval: All closure and drop requests must be approved by the Kai Committee. They will review the request and make a final decision.

For Kai Committee Members

Committee chairs have access to additional management features:

  • View all submitted reports
  • Update report status and deliberation outcomes
  • Add committee notes (visible only to committee)
  • Approve or deny closure requests
  • Archive resolved cases

Best Practices

When Submitting

  • - Be specific about dates and events
  • - Include relevant evidence if available
  • - Focus on facts, not assumptions
  • - Be honest and complete

During the Process

  • - Respect confidentiality
  • - Cooperate with the committee
  • - Be patient as cases are reviewed
  • - Ask questions if unclear
