## 🟢 Article & section maker, and renames, on resolutions

**Asked (Mason, 10-02-26):** "add an article + section maker to the edit
page. Currently you have to just put it straight in as text in the body ...
if someone wants to put in a new article 4 it moves the existing article 4 to
5, 5 to 6, etc." and "a way to edit the name of articles + sections too.
There's no way to."

Until now an amendment could only replace the text of a section that already
existed. A resolution can now also propose:

- **A new article**, in front of any existing article or at the end.
- **A new section**, in front of any section of an article or at its end,
  including inside a new article proposed by the same resolution.
- **A new title** for an article or a section.

**Where.** A new card, "New Articles, Sections & Renames", under the tracked
amendments on both the edit page and the resolution page, with an **Add**
button that opens the maker. Chair and Editor collaborators can use it.
Other people on the edit page see additions appear live.

**Nothing changes until the resolution passes.** Each proposal is listed in
plain words with what it will do — "New article: Constitution Article IV —
Standards Board. Current Articles IV–X become V–XI." — on the edit page, the
resolution page, and the print preview / PDF (new "Structural Changes"
section), so the chapter votes on the renumbering it is getting.

**On pass** (`src/cnb_structure.apply_structure_changes`, in the same
transaction as the amendments): renames, then new articles, then new sections.
An insert takes the number of the item it goes in front of; everything after
it in that document / article moves up one. Articles are Roman numerals,
sections whole numbers. Switched-off (deleted) articles and sections keep
their place and are renumbered with the rest. A retitled section's old title
goes to its revision history.

### Decisions (made by default; Mason did not answer, say if either is wrong)
- **Cross-references are listed, not rewritten.** Each proposal shows the
  references elsewhere in the text that will point at the wrong place after
  the renumbering ("Bylaws Art. VII § 10 cites 'Article VI of the Bylaws'").
  Changing that text takes an amendment; the tool does not edit governing
  text by itself. After a pass, the cross-reference report on the C&B manager
  shows what is left.
- **Applied only on pass**, like every other amendment. It is not a tool for
  restructuring the live document directly.

### Things that protect the documents
- **Passed resolutions keep their citations.** A passed resolution's
  amendments now store the section identifier they were voted on with
  (`ResolutionAmendment.identifier_snapshot`), so a later renumbering does not
  change what an old resolution says it amended, or break its print preview.
  The migration backfills this for every amendment already applied.
- **What can't be renumbered is refused when drafted.** If a later item's
  number is not a Roman numeral / whole number (a section "3a"), an insert in
  front of it is refused with the reason.
- **A pass that can't apply rolls back whole.** If the document changed under
  a proposal after it was drafted, marking the resolution passed applies
  nothing (amendments included), leaves it pending, and says why.

### Known limits
- **Other open resolutions.** A draft or pending resolution whose *body text*
  cites a section by its old number is not updated when another resolution
  renumbers that section. Its tracked amendments follow the section
  correctly; only the typed citation in the body goes stale.
- **The "as of a date" view** shows today's numbering for past dates;
  renumbering is not part of a section's revision history.
- **Removing a section still leaves a gap.** "Remove entire section" switches
  the section off; it does not close up the numbers after it.
- **Direct renames outside a resolution.** The C&B manager can already
  retitle a section directly (Edit section). It still has no direct rename
  for an article; that goes through a resolution.
- Proposals are not locked for simultaneous editing (add/remove only; there
  is no edit-in-place — remove and re-add).

## Deploy
1. `git pull`, then `python manage.py migrate` (0057).
2. Restart `parliament-gunicorn.service`.
3. No CSS rebuild, no schedule change.

⚠️ **multi-chapter:** main's `0057` collides with that branch's migrations
again. After merging main in, run `python manage.py makemigrations --merge`
there.
