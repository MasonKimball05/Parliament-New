## Slating: candidates can't tell from their own status whether they were slated

Approving a slate never showed it to the chapter before voting opened, but a
candidate could still tell where he stood. Putting someone into a slot on
the slate builder (even on a draft, before anything was approved) set his
application to **Slated**, and reviewers could set **Recommended** or **Not
Recommended**. He saw that on My Applications, the slating dashboard and in
the app, and anyone left off still read "Interviewed".

Until voting opens, an applicant now sees:

- **Interviewed** for Recommended, Not Recommended and Slated, once he's had
  his interview (**Submitted** before it), so every candidate reads the same.
- **Submitted** for Under Review.
- Draft, Interview Scheduled, Interviewed and Withdrawn as they are: he
  already knows those.

Once voting opens, he sees his real status. The slating committee always sees
the real statuses on its own pages.

The app's slating screen also offered **Withdraw** during deliberation
whenever the status allowed it, so candidates who weren't slated had the
button and slated ones didn't. It's now offered only while nominations are
open, as it already was on the website.

## Slating: more than two running for an open position

Open nominations already allowed any number of accepted nominees on one
pick-one ballot. Someone wins with at least the round's threshold of the
votes cast for candidates and no tie. With three or more running that often
doesn't happen, and the chair then narrows the field for the next ballot.
The form gave him nothing to go on. Now, on the website's next-round form and
the app's chair screen:

- **Each nominee shows his votes from the last ballot** for that position,
  with the round they're from. Anyone not on that ballot is marked
  **dropped**.
- **Ties for last are flagged** ("tied for last") when two or more on a
  ballot of three or more share the fewest votes. The chapter decides who
  goes on, and the chair unticks the rest.
- **The next ballot starts with whoever was on the last one**, so someone
  already dropped stays off unless the chair ticks him again.
- **At least two stay on the ballot.** A pick-one ballot with a single name
  would elect him on one vote, since abstentions don't count against him, so
  narrowing to one is refused. If the chapter has settled it, the chair
  records that decision instead.

Members' ballots already listed every nominee, on the website and in the app.
A test runs a whole election this way: four nominees, eleven voters, narrowed
round by round until one has a majority.
