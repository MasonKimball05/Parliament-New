Requested by Mason: committee chairs should be able to manage committee
membership all the way up to chairs, not just members/advisors/voters.

## What was actually gating this

Two views back every "+ Add" / "Remove" control on `committee_home.html`:
`committee_add_member` and `committee_remove_member`. Both gated on
`committee.is_vp(request.user) or request.user.is_admin` only — a plain
chair (not also VP, not a site admin) was rejected, **for every role type**,
not just `chair`.

That's worth calling out on its own: `committee_home.py`'s `can_manage`
(which decides whether the "+ Add" buttons and "Remove" links even render)
already includes `is_chair`. So a chair was already being shown a working-
looking "+ Add member" button that silently failed on submit with "You do
not have permission to manage this committee." — a pre-existing bug, not
something this request introduced. Widening the permission check fixes both
at once: it grants the `chair` capability that was asked for, and it makes
the member/advisor/voter controls chairs could already see actually work.

(There's a *separate* page, `/committee/<code>/manage/`, gated by
`@committee_chair_required`, that already let chairs manage members/
advisors/voters — just never chairs. That one wasn't touched; its coverage
was already correct for the roles it handles, and it has no chair-management
action to widen.)

## Fix

Both views now check `committee.is_chair(request.user) or
committee.is_vp(request.user) or request.user.is_admin`.
`committee.is_chair()` — not a raw `.chairs.filter(...).exists()` — to stay
consistent with `committee_chair_required` and every other "chair-level"
check in this codebase. That also means an exec-board committee's plain
members count as chair-level here too (`Committee.is_exec_board`), same as
they do everywhere else "chair-level" is decided — tested explicitly rather
than left as an unstated side effect.

## Worth knowing, not blocking

A chair can now add or remove other chairs on their own committee,
including on the Kai committee — which, given `is_chair()` already grants
full Kai case access everywhere else in this codebase (confirmed in
existing test fixtures: `# chairs get full access`), means a Kai chair can
now anoint or remove a peer chair without going through a VP. This isn't a
new class of escalation — a Kai chair already has full access themselves,
and VP/admin could already do exactly this — it just widens who can invoke
an ability that already existed. Flagging it because this codebase has a
standing rule (the 07-25-26 admin confidentiality boundary work) about
being deliberate anywhere Kai access changes hands; noting it here rather
than silently shipping it.

No guard was added against a chair removing themselves or removing the
last remaining chair on a committee — that gap predates this change (VP
could already do it) and wasn't part of what was asked.
