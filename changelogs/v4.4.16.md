## Scheduled officer transitions can be seen and edited

An officer transition scheduled from a slating period ("Schedule for later")
showed nowhere once it was saved, and editing it was a trap: reopening the
transition form defaulted to "Execute immediately" with the date blank, so
re-saving a change ran the transition at once.

- **Role Transitions** (Officer → Role Transitions) lists every transition
  that's scheduled and hasn't run: the period, when it runs, and each role
  with who gets it and who it replaces. Site admins get **Edit** (the slating
  transition form) and **Cancel transition** (back to Role Transitions after).
  Other officers see it read-only, since only a site admin can run a
  transition.
- **The transition form** opens on the saved schedule: "Schedule for later"
  selected, with the saved date and time filled in, plus a note that
  confirming updates the schedule. Choosing "Execute immediately" ignores the
  filled-in date (a hidden date field still posted, and would have scheduled
  it instead).

### In the app

- `officer/transitions/` gains `scheduled`: each `{period_id, period_name,
  scheduled_at, can_edit, changes: [{position, role, new, removed, already}]}`.
- The app's Role Transitions shows each scheduled transition at the top: when
  it runs and each role's change. A site admin taps it to open the app's
  officer transition (already opening on the saved time, with Cancel); other
  officers see it read-only. Running, rescheduling or cancelling there
  refreshes Role Transitions.

## The Admin v2 dashboard no longer waits a second

The dashboard's Celery card asks the workers whether they're up. Celery's
broadcast ping collects replies until its timeout, so every dashboard load
waited the full second, even with a healthy worker answering in milliseconds.
Measured locally on the demo chapter: 1068 ms before, 34 ms after.

It now stops at the first reply (any worker answering means "up") and keeps
the answer for a minute, so a dead worker costs the one-second wait at most
once a minute. A broker error isn't cached. The app's Site admin home uses the
same card and gets the same speed-up.

The Celery health page still waits the full second. It lists every worker
that answers, so it has to hear from all of them.
