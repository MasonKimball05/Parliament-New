# v3.18.0 — The bylaws the app never implemented

**Date:** 07-31-26
**Type:** Feature batch (governance) + 🔴 authorization fix. **Migration `0011` — includes a data backfill.**
**Deployed:** ✅ 07-31-26

The 07-31-26 Kai committee review. The module was already the most carefully
built thing in Parliament — seven models, per-member permissions, a form
builder, closure requests, read receipts, two releases of confidentiality
hardening. What it was missing was not features but **process**: the chapter
bylaws describe a judicial procedure with recusal, replacement and appeals, and
the app implemented the case file and none of the procedure.

Item 1 is a live authorization hole, not a feature request.

---

## 🔴 1. Recusal — the accused could read, and close, the case against himself

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, `src/view/kai_reports.py`, `src/view/global_search.py`, `src/view/committee/committee_home.py`, `src/view/committee/committee_detail.py`, 3 templates, new `src/test_kai_recusal.py`

The chapter bylaws (§ vi, seeded in `src/management/data/cnb_data.py`):

> *"Should members of the Kai Committee be recused from their duties, the head of
> Kai shall appoint suitable replacement(s) for the position. However, should the
> offenses be separate from each other, then their trials remain separated and
> only the accused must temporarily recuse their seat for their trial."*

**`_get_kai_access(user, committee)` takes a user and a committee and never sees
the report.** Grepped for any comparison of a case party to the requesting user
across `kai_reports.py` and `models/kai.py` — there was none. So a Kai member who
was the accused in an open case could:

- see it in the reviewer list and in global search;
- open it and read the allegation body, and the identity of whoever reported him;
- with `can_edit_open_cases`, set its deliberation outcome;
- with `can_close_cases`, **close the case against himself**;
- export it.

Symmetrically for a reviewer who was the *submitter*. § v of the same bylaws
explicitly contemplates "a member of the Kai Committee is sent to the Kai
Committee for a disciplinary action," so this was not hypothetical.

### The fix, and the property that makes it safe

New `_case_access(user, report, kai_access)` — one chokepoint that narrows
committee-level permissions for one specific case. Every permission goes False
for a party, with `is_recused` / `recusal_reason` attached so callers can explain
the refusal instead of 404ing.

**Recusal is computed from the case (`KaiReport.is_party()`), not from a
`KaiRecusal` row.** Enforcement that depended on a record could be defeated by
failing to create one — two tests assert exactly that, including one that creates
a row, deletes it, and confirms access is still refused. The `KaiRecusal` model
records who filled the vacated seat, which is the *other* half of § vi.

### ⚠️ It was seven live surfaces, not five

`templates/kai/view_reports.html` carried a comment enumerating the five surfaces
that render `KaiReport.description`. **That list was wrong twice in one day:**

| # | Surface | Found |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | list, detail, print, CSV export, global-search card | v3.16.3 |
| 6 | `bulk_actions_kai_reports` — a second CSV export | **v3.17.7** |
| 7 | `committee_home.html` Kai preview | **v3.18.0** |
| 8 | `committee/detail.html` Kai preview — archived, unrouted | **v3.18.0** |

The comment now lists all eight, notes which is dead code, and carries the grep
that produces the list. **An enumeration in a comment is only as good as the grep
behind it, and a list that is confidently wrong is worse than no list.**

### The committee previews were worse than "no recusal check"

Both rendered the **allegation body, the submitter's name and the accused's name
with no `kai_access` gating at all**, keyed only on `is_chair or user.is_admin`.

Two things wrong with that, and the second is the one that matters:

1. No recusal — a chair who was the accused saw his own case here, *including who
   reported him*, which is the single thing the design promises he never sees.
2. **`Committee.is_chair()` returns True for any member of an `is_exec_board`
   committee.** Should Kai ever be flagged exec-board, every exec member would
   read allegation bodies and both parties' identities without holding a single
   `KaiMemberPermission`. This is precisely the bug v3.16.3 fixed in global
   search — gating on committee membership rather than on `_get_kai_access`,
   which is meant to be the single source of truth.

Both now use `_get_kai_access`, exclude recused cases, and gate each field on its
own flag. The archived twin was hardened too, with a test that fails if anyone
re-routes it.

**Status counts were fixed alongside the lists.** A count that includes a case the
list will not show is itself a disclosure: "Pending 4" above three rows tells the
viewer a case about him exists.

## 2. Stand-ins — filling the vacated seat (§§ vi–ix)

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, `src/view/kai_reports.py`, `templates/kai/partials/recusal_panel.html`

Recusal only *removes*. §§ vi–ix also require the seat to be **filled**, and a
stand-in with no `KaiMemberPermission` row can see nothing — so the appointment
has to carry an access grant or it is ceremonial.

- **Who may appoint:** the head of Kai, or a site admin. Deliberately *not* a
  `KaiMemberPermission` flag — appointment hands another member access to a case,
  so it is a delegation of authority, and § vi assigns it to the head of Kai by
  name. A recused member cannot appoint his own replacement.
- **Who may stand in** (Mason 07-31-26): active Members, Chairs, Officers and
  Advisors. Pledges excluded — they cannot sit on a judicial committee — and
  non-`Active` statuses excluded. Also excluded: the parties to the case, anyone
  already recused on it, and anyone already standing in. Appointing a party would
  be undone by `_case_access` immediately, leaving a seat that *looks* filled and
  is empty; refusing it up front fails loudly instead.
- **The grant is a SNAPSHOT**, taken from the vacated seat at the moment of
  appointment and stored on the row. Not a live lookup: if the recused member's
  own permissions are later widened, narrowed, or deleted when he rolls off the
  committee, the stand-in's authority on a case that may already be decided must
  not silently move with it. What the minutes record is what the stand-in had.
- A stand-in holding no committee grant of his own can still reach the case —
  and **only** that case; the list is restricted to his appointments.
- Recusal is checked **before** the stand-in grant, so a hand-written row naming
  a party as their own stand-in cannot resurrect their access.

The `<select>` is built from `eligible_standins()`, and the POST handler
re-checks against the same queryset. **A POST is not a form.**

## 3. Appeals — bylaws § b.i, ten days from notice

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, `src/view/kai_user_dashboard.py`, `templates/kai/partials/appeal_panel.html`, new `src/test_kai_appeals.py`

> *"Kai Committee decisions can be appealed first to the chapter, then to the
> District Chief, and then to the Board of Trustees and the General Convention if
> needed… all Kai Committee appeals must be made within 10 days from the date of
> notice of a decision."*

No appeal model, no appeal state, nothing computing that window. **The anchor
already existed and was already populated:** `accused_notified_at` is literally
"the date of notice of a decision" and had never been used for anything but
display.

- **Ten days is a constant, not a SiteSetting.** It is set by the *General
  Fraternity's* Constitution, not by this chapter, so it is not chapter-
  configurable — same reasoning as `KaiReport.TAG_CHOICES`.
- **A case that never notified the accused has no window** — not an expired one.
  The clock cannot start before notice.
- **`days_remaining` rounds UP.** A right expiring in six hours has one day left,
  not zero; "0 days remaining" while the member can still act is the wrong error
  to make with a deadline.
- `KaiAppeal.can_file()` is the single definition, called by both the template's
  button and the POST handler, so the UI cannot offer what the endpoint refuses.

**The countdown is the point.** A ten-day right nobody is reminded of is a
ten-day right nobody uses.

## 4. Case aging

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, `src/view/kai_reports.py`, `templates/kai/view_reports.html`

Nothing tracked how long a case had been open. `submitted_at` and `reviewed_at`
both existed, so time-to-first-review was computable and never computed — a case
could sit at `pending` indefinitely with no signal anywhere.

`days_open` and `is_stale` (≥ 14 days pending) are properties, so they cost no
extra queries; `oldest_pending` is one query. The list gains a stale badge per
card and a banner naming the oldest pending case with a direct link to it.

## 5. Case assignment

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, `src/view/kai_reports.py`, `templates/kai/partials/assignment_panel.html`

`reviewed_by` is only populated at review time, so before that a case had no
owner at all — on a five-person committee that is how a case sits for three
weeks, which is also why item 4 exists. New `assigned_to`, set independently of
status, plus an **Assignment** filter on the list (Anyone / My cases /
Unassigned) that preserves across the clickable stat cards.

## 6. Per-year case numbers

**Files:** `src/models/kai.py`, migration `0011`, templates

Cases were addressed by raw primary key (`#87`). Sequential PKs leak total case
volume and chronological ordering to anyone who sees one number — the same
join-key concern recorded for `SlatingVote` in `docs/CONFIDENTIALITY_MATRIX.md` —
and `KAI-2026-014` reads better in minutes.

Derived from the highest existing number **for that year**, not from a count, so
deleting a case does not cause the next one to reuse its number. Case numbers
appear in minutes; reuse would be worse than a gap. Existing rows are backfilled
in submission order by migration `0011`.

- **The bulk-action dropdown was offering what the server would refuse.** Every

## 🔴 8. `service-hours/edit/<id>/` has been a hard 500 since v3.0.0

**Files:** `templates/service_hours/edit_submission.html`, `src/templatetags/custom_filters.py`, new `src/test_template_compiles.py`

Found by a final route sweep, not by the feature work. The template uses

```django
{{ existing_responses|get_item:field.id|attr:'text_value' }}
```

and it has **no `{% load %}` tag at all**, while `attr` **was not defined
anywhere in the codebase**.

`Invalid filter` is a `TemplateSyntaxError` raised at **parse** time, not render
time — so the `{% if %}` wrapped around that expression made no difference.
Every request to `edit_service_submission` raised before producing a byte of
output. The route is live (`path('service-hours/edit/<int:submission_id>/', …)`),
so **any member clicking "Edit" on their own service-hours submission got a 500,
from 2026-06-05 until 07-31-26.**

**Fix:** `{% load custom_filters %}`, and `attr` defined next to `get_item` —
Django templates cannot chain attribute access onto a filter result
(`{{ d|get_item:k }}.text_value` is not valid), which is why the filter was
reached for in the first place. It returns `''` rather than raising on a missing
attribute, matching how Django resolves a missing variable.

### Why every existing sweep missed it

- `test_url_smoke` sweeps **zero-argument** pages. This one takes a
  `submission_id`.
- `test_detail_route_smoke` covers parameterised routes but needs a
  `ServiceHoursSubmission` row to reach this one — **the same fixture-starvation
  lesson v3.17.5 paid for** on `/songbook/categories/`: a page that renders zero
  times fails zero times.

### The guard

New `src/test_template_compiles.py` compiles **all 317 templates** in about a
second — no fixtures, no client, no database rows. It cannot catch a runtime
error, but it catches every `TemplateSyntaxError` in the codebase including the
templates no sweep can reach. Verified against the pre-fix state: removing the
`{% load %}` fails the guard with the exact filename and filter.

**A template that no test renders is a template that only your members test.**

Also recorded there: `templates/calendar_subscription_snippet.html` is a
copy-paste scratch file ("Add this to your calendar.html template") containing a
bare `{% endblock %}` inside an HTML comment. It has never compiled and nothing
renders it — allowlisted with that reason rather than deleted, since deleting
someone else's scratch file is not a test's job. **Safe to delete.**

## Dispositioned by Mason 07-31-26 — do not re-propose

- **Kai records are retained deliberately.** The review noted that
  `SlatingInterview` destroys its confidential notes after minutes approval while
  `models/kai.py` has no retention mechanism at all, and proposed adding one.
  **That asymmetry is intentional and the right way round:** the chapter is young,
  precedent is set by case outcomes, and future officers need to understand what
  was decided and why. Losing that has been a severe problem in the past. Kai case
  records are institutional memory.
- **Submissions are deliberately attributable.** `submitted_by` is non-nullable on
  purpose: a report must be serious enough that the reporter will put his name on
  it. The confidentiality promise is not anonymity-from-the-system but that **the
  accused never learns who reported him** — verified 07-31-26 that
  `user_view_report`, `user_dashboard.html` and `user_view_report.html` never
  render `submitted_by`.

Both recorded in CLAUDE.md's do-not-re-flag list.

## Not done — deliberately left

**The full `manage_report.html` partials split (review item §7).** Four new
sections were written as partials, but the pre-existing ~960 lines were not
extracted. Splitting untested existing markup with no way to visually verify the
result — while the reviewer is away — is how a page breaks silently. The
mechanical part is easy; it wants one person looking at the page afterwards.
